## 1. Chosen scenario

**Path:** RouteLogic Velocity (B2B)

The product works for the people who buy it but not for the people who use it every day. That gap between buyer and user is a common and costly B2B problem, and I want to practice solving it.

## 2. The strategic crisis

> If we do nothing for 12 months, the company will likely lose its largest enterprise account to a leaner competitor. That would show the market that a simpler tool can win a RouteLogic customer. Once that's public, other accounts have both a reason and a precedent to trial alternatives at renewal. Frontline usage keeps falling and dispatchers quietly move to workarounds. Our "most powerful operating system" position turns into "the slow, bloated option," and the leaner competitor ends up owning speed, which is the very thing logistics operations buy software for.

## 3. The moment of misery

> The user is currently forced to work around RouteLogic during its most time-sensitive moment. A regional dispatcher has a driver running late or a stop that falls through and must reassign the route in minutes. Instead, they wade through screens and fields built for compliance, finance and executive reporting that have nothing to do with the decision in front of them. Under time pressure, they phone or text the driver directly and track the change on a spreadsheet or whiteboard, then update RouteLogic later, or not at all. The tool meant to speed up the operation has become something they do after the real work is done.

## 4. Problem hook

> We must stop our enterprise customers from defecting to leaner competitors by giving frontline dispatchers a fast, focused core workflow for real-time fleet coordination, with the powerful enterprise features kept available but moved out of their way.

## 5. Value proposition

> For **regional fleet dispatchers and coordinators, the 95% who use RouteLogic every day**, we will **make real-time route coordination the fastest path in the product, using a streamlined core experience that shows only what matters in the moment and moves compliance, finance and reporting into opt-in, role-based layers** because **our largest account is trialing a competitor now, and every week dispatchers spend on workarounds erodes both renewal risk and the data quality the enterprise features depend on.**

## 6. Cold-read self-review

_Read your hook back as a skeptical stakeholder. If you don't feel the urgency, that's your data._

| Question | Your answer |
|---|---|
| Is the business risk high-stakes enough to justify a new initiative? | Yes. Losing our largest account is a direct revenue hit and a public signal to the rest of the base. The board is already worried that complexity is hurting operations, so there is leadership attention to act. |
| Is the moment of misery systemic, or just an edge case? | Systemic. Usage is declining specifically among real-time coordinators, the core daily users, not a niche segment. Reassigning routes under time pressure is a daily, repeated job, not an edge case. _(To validate in Module 2 with user research and usage data.)_ |
| Does the value proposition actually remove the obstacle? | Partly, and that's the risk to test. Decluttering the core workflow removes the friction, but only if we pick the right "essential" features. Enterprise features must stay easy to reach for the buyers who rely on them, or we trade one churn risk for another. |
